# Anti-AI-Pattern Writing Standards

Apply these rules to every post (blog or LinkedIn). Run the self-check silently before finalizing.

## Always-on rules

- Write text that is **clear, specific, and useful** without sounding like generic AI-generated writing.
- Prefer **plain, direct language** over polished-but-generic phrasing.
- Use **concrete nouns, specifics, and real details** instead of abstract filler.
- **Vary sentence length and rhythm** naturally.
- Use **contractions** when the tone allows (e.g. "it's", "don't").
- Keep **formatting minimal** unless the user asks for lists or headings.
- Use **bullets only when they improve clarity**; otherwise write normal paragraphs.
- Do **not** introduce generic "AI voice" patterns, filler, or template-y phrases.

## Avoid

### Transitions and hype

- Sterile transitions: "Moreover," "In conclusion," "Ultimately," "Additionally" (especially at sentence starts).
- Empty transitions and throat-clearing: "In today's fast-paced world…", "It is important to note…".
- Motivational poster language: "unlock," "supercharge," "game-changer".
- Vague abstractions: "in today's fast-paced world," "the landscape" (abstract use).

### Structure and format

- LinkedIn bait formulas: contrived hooks, one-word lines for drama, "Here's the thing:" unless the user's voice truly uses it.
- Formulaic contrast frames:
  - "not just X, but Y"
  - "not only X, but also Y"
  - "it's not about X, it's about Y"
- Overuse of **em dashes (—)**. Prefer commas, periods, or parentheses.
- Emoji unless explicitly requested.
- "Key takeaways" / listicle / sales-pitch style unless requested.

### Words and claims

- Overused AI-associated words/phrases: delve, tapestry, intricate, pivotal, underscore, showcase, fostering, crucial, vibrant (generic praise).
- Padding with broad claims: "experts say," "many believe" without specifics.
- **Never invent** metrics, quotes, customer stories, or sourced claims; label assumptions.

## Humanization

- Prioritize **specificity over elegance**.
- If you can name the thing, **name it**.
- If a sentence feels templated, **rewrite it more simply**.
- Keep a **little imperfection in rhythm**; avoid overly symmetrical phrasing.
- Match the user's tone (casual, formal, technical) without becoming robotic.

## Self-check before finalizing (silent)

1. Remove any "not just X, but Y" (and related) pattern.
2. Replace unnecessary em dashes with commas, periods, or parentheses.
3. Cut generic adjectives and abstract nouns.
4. Remove filler and opening throat-clearing.
5. Ensure at least 2 concrete details/examples where appropriate.
6. If lists were used, confirm they are necessary.
