# Type module — poster copy

**Renders**: `<channel>-poster.md` — the words and their hierarchy. **Never** an image.

This module governs **rendering only**. The angle, Six Questions, outline and claims come from the shared `backbone.md`, and the claim check, anti-AI voice check, refinement loop and `writing-principles.md` all still apply unchanged.

## What you produce

```markdown
## Poster copy — <working title>

**The one message**: <a single sentence; if there are two, this is two posters>
**Where it will be seen**: <and from how far away>

### Hierarchy
1. **Dominant** — <the few words read first, from across the room>
2. **Supporting** — <the line read second, once someone has stopped>
3. **Detail** — <anything read only up close: date, link, qualifier>

**Call to action**: <the one thing to do — or "none, this is awareness">
**What the image has to carry**: <what the words deliberately leave to the visual>
```

## Rules

**One message.** A poster is read in about two seconds by someone walking past. Two messages means neither lands. If the material has two, say so and let the author pick — do not merge them into a longer line.

**Write to the three levels, in order.** Something that must be read from across the room cannot be a sentence. Level 1 is a handful of words; if it needs a clause, it belongs in level 2.

**Say where it will be seen.** A conference wall, a screen in a lobby and a printed A3 next to a desk have completely different reading distances, and the hierarchy is meaningless without one.

**Name what the words leave to the image.** This is the brief for whoever makes the visual. Without it, the poster gets a stock photo that repeats the headline.

**Cut before you shorten.** A poster line that has been squeezed reads as squeezed. Drop the idea instead.

## What this must never do

- Generate, describe in detail, or reference a rendered image file.
- Specify typefaces, colours, or layout — that is design, and it is not this system's job.
- Invent a statistic to make a headline land.
- Add a QR code, hashtag, or handle the author did not ask for.
