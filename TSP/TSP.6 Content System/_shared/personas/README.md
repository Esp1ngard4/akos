# Personas

Four ways of building a piece. Each is a **craft** model: structure, rhythm, how the opening earns the next line, how the piece closes. None of them is a source of substance — that boundary is principle 2 in [`../writing-principles.md`](../writing-principles.md), and it binds all four.

| Persona | File | Use when the piece is… |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflective Narrator** | [`reflective-narrator.md`](reflective-narrator.md) | something that happened, and what it changed |
| **Plain Explainer** | [`plain-explainer.md`](plain-explainer.md) | how a thing works, or what it is |
| **Argument Builder** | [`argument-builder.md`](argument-builder.md) | a position being reasoned toward |
| **Direct Copywriter** | [`direct-copywriter.md`](direct-copywriter.md) | short, and has to earn attention line by line |

## How one gets picked

At **Phase 1**, alongside the angle. The skill reads the material, proposes **one** persona with a one-line reason, and the author confirms or overrides. Recorded in that post's `backbone.md`.

One persona per piece. Blending them produces a piece with no shape at all, which is the failure mode that generic AI writing already occupies.

## Choosing well

The question is **what the material already is**, not what would be impressive.

- The author reports something that happened → Reflective Narrator.
- The author explains a mechanism → Plain Explainer.
- The author reasons toward a position they're still testing → Argument Builder.
- The piece is short and competing for attention → Direct Copywriter.

**The trap, and it's the one that already bit this system once (2026-08-08):** picking a persona whose shape is more interesting than the material, then filling the gap with invented content. Argument Builder is the most dangerous here, because its shape is the most satisfying and it needs a genuine open question to run on. If the author has already decided, it is the wrong persona — no amount of craft makes a settled decision into a live argument, and trying manufactures doubt the author doesn't have.

**Reflective Narrator is the right default when unsure.** It demands the least material the author didn't already supply.

## Adding a persona

Only when a real piece doesn't fit any of the four. Each file states: what it's for, where it comes from, how the piece is built, what the opening and closing do, and — the section that matters most — **what it must not do**, which is where the collision with the principles gets written down before it happens.
