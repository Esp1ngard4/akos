# How much of the work each piece needs

What to expect of a piece of work, from finding out to delivering it. These are guidelines, not gates: they say which stages usually help and how deep to go, so a team member is not left guessing and is not made to write documents nobody reads. Each stage has its own guideline, in `stages/`; a project that wants more of one tightens that file in its own copy of the skill.

| Size | Typical work | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Small | A fix or a known change, well understood, done by one person in a day or so | Straight to implement. A few lines of notes: what was done, and how it was checked |
| Medium | A feature with a clear problem and some unknowns | A short specification: what will be built and its acceptance criteria, with the plan as a checklist in the same document. Discovery only if something needs finding out first |
| Large | New capability, real unknowns, or several people | Each stage its own documents, as many as the work needs: discovery, specification, a plan agreed before building, then implement |

The size is a judgement, not a measure. The agent proposes one from the WBS row (its estimate, its description, whether it has children, how much is unknown) and the person decides. When the work turns out bigger than thought, it moves up a size: nothing done so far is wasted.

A size says which stages a piece of work needs, never how many documents a stage may hold. Research that takes three documents gets three.

## When the work shows a stage was wrong

Go back a stage: the specification or the plan is revised, and agreed again. A note says what changed and why.
