# TD.15 — Team Status Board

## Purpose

Where the team updates the status of its own work, and how that comes back into the project's WBS. A team that shares a WBS needs to say what moved without writing the register: the board is a workbook per sprint the team edits itself, or through Copilot in Excel, and the product manager consolidates it into the WBS at the weekly review and the close-out. It answers what the WBS alone cannot for a team: how does everyone's status reach one register without everyone writing it?

It is a check, not a ceremony. Planning, the weekly review and the close-out each have a board step only where this tool is installed. A team that works in the WBS only, through the agent, never installs it; a team on another tool adapts how it reads.

## What the skill covers (and this document doesn't repeat)

`status-board-manager/SKILL.md` holds the board's sheets and columns, the three moments it is written, the consolidation's proposals and what decides each, and where each board step sits in the ceremonies. This document covers why the tool is shaped as it is, how to adapt it, and its history.

## Design decisions

**The register keeps one writer.** The WBS is one file, and a register several people save at once loses changes. The team writes the board; the product manager, through the agent, writes the WBS. The board is never the register, and the WBS is never edited from the board without a person confirming the change.

**Consolidation proposes; a person decides.** Reading the board is mechanical, bringing it in is not: a row marked done has acceptance criteria someone must confirm, new work has an effect on the sprint someone must state, and a blocked row needs an owner. So the tool proposes row by row and every write goes through the WBS tool's own commands, with their gates.

**One board per sprint, the sprint's work only.** A board that carries the backlog becomes a second WBS that drifts. Each sprint starts clean from what planning committed, and the closed one goes to the archive once it has been consolidated, so its last state is kept and the folder holds one live board.

**The board follows the WBS's commitments.** A pull-in, a carried split or a cancellation agreed with the product manager reaches the board the same day, so the team never works from a board that is behind the sprint.

**Written rarely, and carefully.** The tool writes the board only to set it up, to follow a commitment, and to end a consolidation. Each write re-reads it first and keeps every cell the team entered, so an update made since the last read survives. The file's version history covers an edit made in the same moment.

**A step where it is installed, nothing where it is not.** There is no setting to turn the board on: the ceremonies look for the tool and a board, and say nothing when there is none. What a team works in is the team's decision; the tools follow it.

**Notes are a dated log, and they reach the WBS one by one.** A row's Notes hold one entry per line, newest on top, each with its date and author. The cell then shows how the work evolved, and the consolidation brings each entry since the last one into the WBS row as a dated note of its own, so several comments on one row never merge into one. An entry the row already holds is recognised and not brought in twice, so a note written on a consolidation's own day is neither lost nor doubled.

**The board holds tasks; the WBS holds deliverables.** A team splits a WBS row into the pieces it actually works on, and those pieces come and go within a sprint. So each board row is a task naming the WBS row it serves, a WBS row may have several, and the consolidation rolls them up: a row starts when any of its tasks moves, closes when all of them are done, with the weakest verdict among them, and carries every task's notes, each named by its task. The WBS never grows a row per task.

**Leaves on the board, parents with the product manager.** A committed parent is worked through its children, and closing it means its own acceptance criteria are met, which is the product manager's judgment. The board shows the work the team does.

## Naming conventions

- **The board:** `Status Board <scope> <sprint>.xlsx`, beside the WBS. Recognised by its *About* sheet, not its name.
- **Board statuses:** To do · Doing · Done · Blocked · Cancelled. **AC verdicts:** met · partly met · not met.
- **Columns:** *WBS ID*, *WBS item*, *Task*, *Owner*, *Status*, *Notes*, *AC verdict*, *Updated on*, *Updated by*.
- **Notes:** `YYYY-MM-DD · Name: text`, one entry per line, newest on top. On the WBS row a board note reads `On the team board, <date>, <name> (<task>): <text>`.
- **Dates** on the board as `YYYY-MM-DD`; the WBS writes them its own way (`2026-Oct-27`), and the tool converts.

## Adapting it to your tools

**Every real implementation will need its own changes to work properly.** Only the reading depends on the tool the team updates: the proposals, the decisions and every write to the WBS stay as they are. The installer records an adapted skill as a local change, so it stays reconcilable with upstream.

### A team that works in the WBS only

**Where it plugs in:** nowhere. Don't install the tool. Team members tell the agent what moved, and the agent writes the WBS through `wbs-manager`, one writer at a time. The ceremonies have no board step.

### A Copilot agent that updates the board

**Where it plugs in:** the team's side, before any of the tool's steps: how a team member's update reaches the board.

**What it takes over:** a Microsoft 365 Copilot agent (or any chat agent with access to the file) can take an update in plain words, such as "the login form is done, all criteria met, released to test", and write it into the board the way the conventions say: the right task row, a dated note on top, a status and a verdict from the lists.

**What to watch for:** its instructions are the board's *How to update* sheet, which the tool regenerates with every board; point the agent at that sheet, or copy it word for word, rather than writing a second set of rules that drifts. It writes the board only, never the WBS: the consolidation stays the product manager's. Build it once a team has run a few sprints and Copilot in Excel alone is not enough.

### Microsoft Planner

**Where it plugs in:** *propose*'s reading, and the three writes.

**What it takes over:** the board itself. Planner tasks carry the WBS ID in their title or a label; buckets or the progress field map to the board statuses. Planner suits a team that already lives in Teams, and Copilot can report on a plan.

**What to watch for:** the agent needs a connector to read Planner (the Graph API), which an Excel file does not. Keep the WBS ID on every task, or the consolidation cannot match it.

### Microsoft Lists, Jira, GitHub Projects

**Where it plugs in:** the same two places.

**What it takes over:** a list or project whose items carry the WBS ID and a status the tool can map to its five.

**What to watch for:** a tool with its own workflow states needs a mapping to To do · Doing · Done · Blocked, written into your copy of the skill. Two-way sync is not the design: the board follows the WBS's commitments, and the WBS takes the board's status, each through its own step.

## How it is tested

`status-board-manager/scripts/smoke_test.py` builds a scratch project with a sprint running today and checks: set-up writes the sprint's leaves and archives a consolidated board, refusing one that was not; the workbook reads back what was written, including shared strings and date cells as Excel saves them; tasks roll up per WBS row: one open task keeps a row from closing, all done closes it with the weakest verdict and each task's newest note, a moving task starts it; blocked, unlinked, back, reopened, missing and unknown are each proposed; dated notes become WBS notes oldest first, none brought in twice; a board with the older *Title* and *Note* columns still reads; *sync* adds a pull-in and marks a cancellation, keeping the team's cells; *link* writes a new row's ID; the consolidation stamp. `tests/smoke_test.py` runs it, installs the skill, and checks the status report says how fresh the WBS is.

What is **not** tested is how a team uses it: whether people keep the board current, or whether a week between consolidations is right for them. Those are the retro's questions.

## Maintenance

**Each sprint:** set up at planning, consolidated at the weekly review and at the close-out, archived at the next set-up.

**When a commitment changes mid-sprint:** the board is updated the same day.

**When the team changes tool:** adapt *propose*'s reading and the three writes in your copy of the skill.
